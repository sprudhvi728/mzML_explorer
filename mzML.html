Interpretation notes

RT range reflects the length of your LC gradient — typical shotgun proteomics runs span 30–120 min. Very short ranges (< 5 min) may indicate a targeted or isocratic method.

MS1 vs MS2 scans: in data-dependent acquisition (DDA), each MS1 survey scan triggers several MS2 fragmentation events. An MS2/MS1 ratio of 3–10× is typical; very low ratios may suggest a low-complexity sample or a non-DDA method.

Max TIC gives a rough sense of signal dynamic range. Large variation between scans is normal — TIC drops between elution peaks and during gradient washout.

Chromatogram

Interpretation notes

TIC (Total Ion Current) traces the sum of all ion intensities over time. Broad peaks indicate co-eluting compounds; sharp peaks suggest well-resolved chromatography. Baseline noise between peaks is normal.

Base peak chromatogram (BPC) plots only the most intense ion in each scan. It often gives a cleaner view of individual peaks than TIC because it is less affected by chemical background.

Click any point on the chromatogram to jump directly to that scan's spectrum in the table and spectrum viewer below.

Spectrum MS2
Interpretation notes

The m/z axis (x) represents mass-to-charge ratio of detected ions. The intensity axis (y) represents ion abundance. Peaks correspond to individual ionic species.

In an MS2 spectrum, peaks are fragment ions produced by breaking the precursor peptide or molecule. The pattern of fragments encodes sequence information used by database search engines (e.g. Mascot, MaxQuant, Sequest) to identify peptides.

In an MS1 spectrum, you see the intact ion population eluting at that retention time. Isotope envelopes (clusters of peaks spaced ~1 Da apart) are normal for multiply-charged peptide ions. Use the plotly toolbar to zoom into regions of interest.
